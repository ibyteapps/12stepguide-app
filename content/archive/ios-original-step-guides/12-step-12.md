# Step 12

*Having had a spiritual awakening as the result of these steps, we tried to carry this message to alcoholics, and to practice these principles in all our affairs*.

This is the last but not the least of the steps. Step 12 is made up of three parts. The first part '***Having had a spiritual awakening as the result of these steps***' is a promise that will come true as a result of the first 11 steps.

The second part is '***we tried to carry this message to alcoholics***'. One might wonder why this is required, now that we have got our sobriety. But the greatest paradox of this gift of sobriety is that to keep it, you must keep giving it away.

And the last bit '***and to practice these principles in all our affairs***' which suggests that we try to practice this principles as we go about our day to day life.

Pretty much self-explanatory once you break down the step. However, this is an important step. I was a few years sober before I even realized that the second part of this step '*we tried to carry this message to alcoholics*' does not use the word 'practicing' alcoholics. It simply uses the word 'alcoholics'. I then understood that whenever I was sharing with any alcoholic, be it my sponsor, be it anywhere, I was carrying the message of recovery with me all the time. When I was sharing at meetings, be it even just for 2 minutes, I was carrying the message.

I was gradually guided into service by my sponsor and I started working with other alcoholics a little by little. One important thing I learnt about 12 stepping practicing alcoholics was that I could not stop anyone else's drinking but mine. I was powerless over their illness, I had only gained the power of 24 hours over my illness.

So when anyone asks me if I can help them stop drinking, my answer back to them is I cannot. But if they get to a meeting, they might just hear something that might get them through that day and then they can start all over again the next day.

I also learnt that when I'm passing the message, I must only talk about my own drinking. It is my experience, strength and hope that are of the most value to other alcoholics. My opinions are mine and I must keep them to myself.

Another important fact I've learnt is that I cannot get through to a person who is not sober. They might perhaps not remember having any conversation with anyone when they sober up. It's best to let them sober up first and then to talk to them.

It is also imperative to practice absolute honesty with myself and my fellows in all of my affairs. Practicing the 12 steps is a job of a lifetime and I cannot stress how much I wanted to be perfect at them and declare in meetings that I had completed my steps in my early days. It took me well over 3 years to actually say that I had taken the steps and was indeed taking them everyday after that.

I also went on to study the 12 traditions and the 12 concepts with my sponsor over a period of time. I'm happy to say that I'm not perfect, but I'm finally okay to be in my own skin and I love my life. I hope my experience, strength and hope has helped you and that you will take your 12 steps with your higher power and your sponsor.
