# Introduction

This app is intended as a guide for anyone wanting to work the 12 steps of alcoholics anonymous. This is an account of my experience with the steps. The language is plain and simple to enable this app to help a maximum number of people.

Hi everyone, my name is not important and I'm an *alcoholic*. I've been sober for x number of years now and I have had the same sponsor for a few years. I've been wanting to write this app for a while. My experience, strength and hope are free of cost and you can always get in touch with me at ibyteapps@gmail.com for more fellowship. I'll be more than grateful to have you as a friend.

I've also developed many other recovery related apps which can be found in the apple store by [clicking here.](https://itunes.apple.com/gb/developer/tushar-bhagat/id901932809) (Opens App Store) .

Some of them are not free and if you cannot afford to purchase them, I'd be happy to give you a free upgrade voucher on most of them. But if you can, I'd kindly urge you to purchase my apps so that I can also continue to write more apps and help many more, who cannot afford purchase them.

If you are on facebook, can you please like my page here: [https://www.facebook.com/12steptoolkit](https://www.facebook.com/12steptoolkit)

Let's start with the preamble.

**AA Preamble**: **Alcoholics Anonymous** is a fellowship of men and women who share their experience, strength and hope with each other that they may solve their common problem and help other to recover from **alcoholism**. The only requirement for **AA** membership is a desire to stop drinking.

This bit is extremely important and for me as it encompasses everything there is in recovery. Especially the word *share* in our preamble is of utmost significance. Throughout this app, I'll be sharing with you and I won't be telling you anything. For every person ought to decide what they want for themselves. I've had a bitter experience with a sponsor in my early days who wanted to tell me how I should be living my life, how many meetings I should get to every week, whether I should work or not, which people were good for me in the fellowship etc. Later on, this sponsor sacked me because he felt I was not being honest with him. I later found out that not many fellow members had no respect for this sponsor of mine as he had only put the drink down, but his life was unmanageable just as before. He later made amends to me as he had used atrocious language when he had sacked me. He remains sober and I still consider him my friend. But he no longer is my sponsor.

After waiting for a few days, I asked someone else to sponsor me and I shared my experience about being sacked. My new (current sponsor) told me something very profound. He said that sponsorship was a friendship and not a dictatorship. He also said that it was a two way relationship and that I would benefit from him as much as he would benefit from me. He's been a good friend of mine throughout the years and we have enjoyed many ups and downs of life together. My sponsor never tells me what to do when I call him with a problem. Instead he will share his experience of a similar incidence in his life and if he hasn't had that experience, he'll probably say 'I don't know, but you can pray and ask your own higher power'.

Having a sponsor is very important for my own recovery. If you do not have one, don't panic. I was suggested that I get to as many meetings as I can and look for the similarities. And in choosing a sponsor, I was suggested that I find someone who had worked the 12 steps and had something that I wanted. I've always craved for peace of mind and my current sponsor always seemed to have it. You need to look into your own soul and find out what you want from your sponsor. In other words, find someone who you want to be like. Let's get started with the steps.
