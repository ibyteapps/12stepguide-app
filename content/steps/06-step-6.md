# Step 6: Were entirely ready to have God remove all these defects of character.

Step 6 is often called a pivotal moment in the Twelve Step program, yet it can be easy to underestimate its significance. It might seem like a simple step at first glance—just being "ready" to have our character defects removed. But as the *Twelve Steps and Twelve Traditions* (12 & 12) book says, this step "separates the men from the boys," implying that it requires a level of willingness and surrender that not everyone is ready to embrace. It took me a while to grasp the true meaning of this, but after several years in the program and countless meetings, I realized that putting the drink down and making spiritual progress are two very different things.

Many people in recovery make the mistake of thinking that simply not drinking is enough. But as I came to understand, not drinking is just the beginning—it’s the foundation, not the entire structure. For real, lasting recovery, we need to dig deeper and confront the flaws and defects that have shaped our lives. Step 6 is about acknowledging that while putting down the drink is vital, it is equally important to work on the aspects of our character that led us to drink in the first place. Without this work, sobriety can become a dry, joyless existence, and we risk falling back into old patterns of anger, resentment, and dissatisfaction.

## The Difference Between Abstinence and Recovery

In my journey, I’ve met people who attend meetings religiously—seven days a week—but are not truly working the program. They’ve stopped drinking, but they’re not making any progress in their spiritual lives. These individuals are often miserable, angry, and difficult to be around. It was a stark contrast to others who had only been sober for a few months but were actively working the steps, addressing their character defects, and making real changes in their lives. These people seemed genuinely happy, at peace with themselves, and were a joy to be around.

What I came to realize is that this program is about much more than just putting down the bottle. It's a program of change—a journey of personal growth and spiritual development. Putting down the drink is just the tip of the iceberg. Beneath the surface lies a wealth of unresolved issues, defects of character, and emotional baggage that must be addressed if we hope to maintain long-term sobriety. Step 6 is where we become willing to let go of these old ways of thinking and behaving, where we prepare ourselves for the deep, internal transformation that comes with continued spiritual growth.

## Understanding Our Defects of Character

Step 4 gave us a detailed inventory of our character defects—the traits and behaviors that have caused harm to ourselves and others. By the time we reach Step 6, we should have a good sense of what those defects are. But being aware of our flaws is only half the battle. Step 6 asks us to go further—to be entirely ready to have those defects removed. This means letting go of the comfort we’ve found in those behaviors, even if they’ve been destructive. For many of us, our defects of character have become like old friends. We’ve relied on them to navigate life, even though they’ve often led us down the wrong path.

In the *12 & 12*, seven deadly sins are mentioned: Pride, Greed, Lust, Anger, Gluttony, Envy, and Sloth. These sins may manifest differently for each of us, but they are universal aspects of human nature. We will never be entirely free of them—they are part of being human. However, Step 6 isn’t about perfection. It’s about becoming willing to work on these defects, to allow our Higher Power to remove the hold they have over us. For example, pride may show up in our need to be right all the time, greed in our desire for more than we need, or anger in our inability to forgive. These character defects keep us stuck in old patterns, but by becoming willing to let them go, we open ourselves up to real change.

## Becoming Ready for Transformation

In essence, Step 6 is about willingness. We’re not required to remove our defects ourselves—that’s the work of our Higher Power. But we must be entirely ready to let them go. For many of us, this is where the challenge lies. We may recognize that certain behaviors are harmful, but are we really ready to give them up? Are we prepared to live a life free of the crutches we’ve relied on for so long? If we find ourselves resistant, Step 6 offers a simple solution: pray for the willingness to come. Even if we’re not entirely ready, we can ask our Higher Power to help us become willing.

For me, this process took time. Some of my character defects were so ingrained in my identity that the thought of letting them go felt terrifying. What would life be like without my anger to protect me? How would I cope without my pride driving me to succeed? But through prayer and reflection, I began to see that these defects were not serving me—they were holding me back. My anger didn’t protect me; it isolated me. My pride didn’t help me succeed; it prevented me from connecting with others. The more willing I became to let go, the more peace I began to find.

## Working on Character Defects or Relapsing

As the saying goes, "We’re either working on our recovery, or we’re working on a relapse." This is especially true when it comes to Step 6. If we’re not actively working on our character defects, those defects will continue to run our lives. And when that happens, the risk of relapse increases dramatically. For me, working on my defects of character has been a continuous process. It’s not something I did once and then moved on from. Instead, it’s a daily commitment to self-improvement and spiritual growth.

Each day, I try to identify the defects that are still showing up in my life and ask my Higher Power for help in removing them. Sometimes, the progress is slow. Other times, I can feel the shift almost immediately. But no matter what, I keep moving forward, trusting that as long as I stay willing, my Higher Power will continue to guide me toward greater serenity and freedom.

## The Power of Step 6 in Long-Term Recovery

Step 6 is often where we begin to see the deeper work of recovery take shape. It’s the point where sobriety moves from simply "not drinking" to a full-fledged program of spiritual growth. While putting down the drink is essential, it’s not enough to ensure long-term recovery. We must continue to work on ourselves, to confront the behaviors and attitudes that led us to drink in the first place.

In my experience, Step 6 has been one of the most transformative steps in the program. It’s the moment where I became fully committed to the process of change—where I stopped clinging to my old ways of thinking and behaving and opened myself up to the possibility of a new way of life. It’s not always easy, but it’s absolutely worth it. The freedom that comes from letting go of character defects and embracing a life of humility, compassion, and spiritual growth is beyond anything I could have imagined when I first entered recovery.

## Practical Tips for Working Step 6

If you’re approaching Step 6, my advice is to take your time and be gentle with yourself. Becoming entirely ready to have your defects of character removed can feel overwhelming, especially if you’re deeply attached to some of those behaviors. Remember, this is a process. You don’t have to be perfect, and you don’t have to be ready all at once. Start by identifying the defects you’re willing to let go of, and pray for the willingness to address the others.

It’s also important to keep an open line of communication with your sponsor and your Higher Power. Talk about the defects you’re struggling with and ask for guidance. The more you lean into this process, the more you’ll find that your willingness grows.

Finally, remember that Step 6 is just one part of a larger journey. It’s not about reaching some final destination of perfection—it’s about progress, one day at a time. As long as you stay willing to work on your defects and ask your Higher Power for help, you’re on the right path.
