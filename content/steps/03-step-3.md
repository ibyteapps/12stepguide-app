# Step 3: Made a decision to turn our will and our lives over to the care of God as we understood Him.

Step 3 is often considered the pivotal moment in the Twelve Step program—a decision that marks a profound turning point. Unlike Step 1 and Step 2, Step 3 is a working step, meaning it requires deliberate action on our part. It’s not just about understanding; it’s about committing ourselves to a new way of life. This step is about surrendering—not in defeat, but in trust. We make a decision to turn over control to a Power greater than ourselves, trusting that this Power will guide us to sanity and wholeness. And it is a decision we make each day, because recovery is lived one day at a time.

I emphasize the word *today* because this is a program based on 24 hours at a time. Every morning, I make this decision again—to turn my will and my life over to the care of a God of my own understanding. I know I don’t have to make this decision perfectly, but I do have to make it consistently. I remind myself that all I need is a little willingness, and from that willingness, the decision becomes easier to embrace. It doesn’t require perfection, just sincerity.

## Breaking Down Step 3: Will and Lives

There are two key concepts in this step: *will* and *lives*. Our *will* refers to our thinking—our desires, plans, and intentions. It’s about our internal world: the thoughts, urges, and emotions that guide us. When we turn our will over to our Higher Power, we are essentially saying, “I trust that You know better than I do. Please guide my thinking so that I’m no longer ruled by my fears, obsessions, and selfish desires.”

*Lives* refers to our actions, how we conduct ourselves in the external world. This is where our decisions manifest—how we treat others, how we handle responsibilities, and how we react to challenges. Turning our lives over means surrendering control of our actions, asking our Higher Power to guide us in the right direction. In other words, we are no longer living according to our self-will or selfish motives. Instead, we seek guidance on how to act in ways that align with the principles of recovery: honesty, humility, compassion, and service to others.

When I came to understand this, Step 3 began to make more sense. It wasn’t about giving up my identity or becoming passive. It was about letting go of the need to control everything and everyone around me. It was about trusting that there was a path laid out for me that would lead to serenity if I would just stop trying to force my will onto situations and let my Higher Power take the reins.

## Struggling to Find a Higher Power

For those who struggle with the concept of a Higher Power, Step 3 can feel intimidating. How can you turn your will and life over to something you’re not even sure you believe in? It’s important to remember that Step 3 is not about having everything figured out. It’s about willingness—the willingness to believe that there might be a power greater than you and the willingness to let go of control.

When I first came to this step, I didn’t fully understand or have a clear image of my Higher Power. But I was told that wasn’t necessary. I was encouraged to start small. My sponsor suggested that I pray every day, even if I wasn’t sure who or what I was praying to. The simple act of asking for help, of seeking guidance outside of myself, was enough to begin. Over time, the answers came, and with them came a growing sense of peace and clarity. Even people who have been sober for decades sometimes say they don’t fully understand their Higher Power, but they continue to pray because it works.

For me, my conscience became my Higher Power in the early stages of my recovery. When I didn’t know what to do, I would ask my conscience what the next right thing was. The answers weren’t always immediate, but they came. And each time I acted on those answers, my life improved in small but noticeable ways. Over time, these small decisions accumulated, and I found that my life was far better than it had ever been—even before I started drinking.

## Taking Step 3 with a Sponsor

When I was ready to take this step, I did it with my sponsor by my side. He asked me to get on my knees, and he did the same. Together, we said a prayer, and I made the decision to turn my will and my life over to my Higher Power. That moment was significant for me. It was a physical act that mirrored the spiritual surrender I was making. Of course, you don’t have to take Step 3 in the same way I did. Some people say a simple prayer in the privacy of their own homes. Others do it in a quiet moment of reflection. There’s no right or wrong way to do this step, but for me, kneeling down with my sponsor made the commitment feel real.

## The Power of Prayer in Step 3

The AA Big Book suggests a simple prayer for those taking Step 3:

"*God, I offer myself to Thee—to build with me and to do with me as Thou wilt. Relieve me of the bondage of self, that I may better do Thy will. Take away my difficulties, that victory over them may bear witness to those I would help of Thy Power, Thy Love, and Thy Way of life. May I do Thy will always*."

This prayer is a way of vocalizing the decision to turn your will and life over to a Higher Power. It acknowledges that we are powerless over our own selfish desires and asks for guidance in overcoming them. But if this prayer doesn’t resonate with you, don’t worry. The Big Book emphasizes that this is only a suggested prayer. God, or your Higher Power, listens to the language of the heart. In the early days of my recovery, I didn’t have any fancy words. All I could say was, “Please help me, God.” That simple prayer was enough.

Step 3 is about action. It’s about turning your will and life over every single day. It’s about seeking guidance, even when you don’t know what the answer will be. It’s about trusting the process, even when you don’t fully understand it. And most importantly, it’s about surrendering to the idea that you don’t have to do this alone.

## Experiencing the Results of Step 3

One of the most powerful lessons I learned from Step 3 is that life gets easier when I stop trying to control it. When I trust that my Higher Power has my best interests at heart, I am freed from the burden of micromanaging every aspect of my life. Decisions become clearer, relationships improve, and I find peace in situations that used to cause me stress and anxiety.

Over time, I’ve come to see that the decision to turn my will and life over isn’t a one-time event. It’s a daily practice. Each morning, I recommit to this step, knowing that as long as I remain willing, my Higher Power will guide me. It doesn’t mean life is perfect or that I won’t face challenges. But it does mean that I no longer face those challenges alone. I have a Higher Power that I trust to show me the way.

## “God helps those who help themselves.”

This old saying is true in the context of Step 3. We turn our will and lives over to our Higher Power, but that doesn’t mean we sit back and do nothing. We must remain active in our recovery—taking the steps, helping others, and doing the next right thing. In return, our Higher Power provides us with the strength, wisdom, and clarity we need to continue on this journey.

Step 3 is not the end of the road; it’s the beginning of a new way of living. By turning our will and lives over to the care of God as we understand Him, we open ourselves to a life that is no longer driven by fear, selfishness, or obsession. Instead, we live with purpose, guided by a power greater than ourselves.
