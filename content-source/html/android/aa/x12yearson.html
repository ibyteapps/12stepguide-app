Twelve Years On: A Journey of Sobriety, Family, and Growth

Twelve years ago, I embarked on a journey of sobriety that changed my life in ways I couldn’t have imagined. Back then, my only goal was to stop drinking and save myself from the chaos and destruction alcohol had caused in my life. What I didn’t expect was the depth of transformation I would experience, not only in myself but also in my relationships, my career, and my understanding of the world. Sobriety has given me far more than just freedom from alcohol; it has opened up a life filled with meaning, growth, and an ever-deepening connection to those around me.

Family First: A Foundation of Recovery

One of the most important lessons I learned early on in my recovery was the value of family. My first sponsor always reminded me to focus on my children and to be present in their lives. "They grow up fast," he used to say, and he was right. Today, my children are adults, and while they don’t remember much about my drinking, they do recall that I used to drink a lot. The fact that they don’t carry vivid memories of those darker times is a gift I attribute to my commitment to sobriety.

As I navigated the early years of my recovery, I made a conscious effort to put my children’s needs ahead of my own. I wanted to ensure that they grew up to be responsible, kind, and capable individuals—good citizens of the world. Two of them are now working in qualified jobs, while my youngest is currently at university. Watching them flourish into independent adults has been one of the greatest rewards of my sobriety.

Eight years ago, I remarried, and with that came the responsibility of caring for my step-children, who are now in their late teens. Just like with my biological children, I have made it a point to guide and support them as they navigate life’s challenges. Being a sober parent has allowed me to be fully present for all of them, and for that, I am deeply grateful.

Exploring Spirituality: The Journey Within

In addition to strengthening my relationships with my family, my journey in sobriety has also been marked by a deep exploration of spirituality. One of the guiding principles of Alcoholics Anonymous (A.A.) is the idea of seeking a higher power and expanding our spiritual horizons. The Big Book encourages us to explore different spiritual paths, and I’ve taken that advice to heart.

One of the most impactful spiritual practices I’ve incorporated into my life is Vipassana meditation. Over the past several years, I have completed several 10-day meditation retreats, a 20-day retreat, and I am currently planning to participate in a 30-day retreat in 2025. Vipassana has been transformative for me, helping me cultivate mindfulness, inner peace, and a deeper connection to my higher power. For those who are curious about Vipassana, I highly recommend visiting Dhamma.Org, where you can learn more about the practice and find retreats near you.

The benefits of Vipassana have extended far beyond the meditation cushion. This practice has helped me become more patient, compassionate, and grounded in my daily life. It has also given me a sense of clarity and purpose, allowing me to navigate life’s challenges with a calm and focused mind. Exploring spirituality has been a crucial part of my sobriety, and I encourage others to find their own spiritual path—whether through meditation, prayer, or other practices.

The Importance of Travel: Broadening Horizons

One of the many gifts sobriety has given me is the opportunity to travel the world with my wife and children. Together, we have visited all seven continents, and each trip has been an adventure filled with discovery, learning, and personal growth. I’m not sharing this to boast, but rather to highlight what is possible when you stay sober and live life fully.

Traveling has broadened my horizons in ways I could never have imagined. It has allowed me to experience different cultures, meet people from all walks of life, and gain a deeper appreciation for the beauty and diversity of our planet. More importantly, it has given me the opportunity to bond with my family in meaningful ways. Whether we’re hiking through the mountains of South America, exploring ancient temples in Asia, or marveling at the wildlife in Africa, these shared experiences have brought us closer together and created memories that will last a lifetime.

I truly believe that travel is one of the most enriching experiences a person can have. It challenges you to step outside of your comfort zone, opens your mind to new perspectives, and fosters a sense of connection to the world around you. In sobriety, I have been able to travel with a clear mind and an open heart, and I encourage others to explore the world in whatever way they can.

Self-Sufficiency and Responsibility

One of the core values I’ve carried with me throughout my sobriety is self-sufficiency. This value was instilled in me early on, and it has remained an integral part of my life. I’ve continued to work in my field of passion, finding fulfillment in my career while also balancing my role as a parent and step-parent. I’ve also made it a priority to instill this same value in my children and step-children, teaching them the importance of self-reliance, good eating habits, and taking care of their physical and mental health.

For me, self-sufficiency goes beyond financial independence; it’s about being responsible for your well-being in all areas of life. It means taking ownership of your choices, your actions, and your health. In sobriety, I’ve learned to take care of my body and mind through regular exercise, healthy eating, and a commitment to mental wellness. This has allowed me to maintain balance and resilience as I navigate life’s ups and downs.

Building Healthy Relationships: From Divorce to Harmony

One of the unexpected challenges I faced early in sobriety was my divorce, which occurred shortly after I stopped drinking. At the time, I was heartbroken, but I soon realized that sobriety gave me the tools I needed to handle the situation with grace and maturity. Over the years, I’ve worked hard to maintain a congenial relationship with my ex-partner, and today, we are able to discuss life issues and even share experiences without animosity.

This ability to foster healthy, respectful relationships is something I attribute directly to my work in A.A. The program taught me to let go of resentment, practice forgiveness, and focus on living in the present. These principles have allowed me to build positive relationships, not just with my ex-partner, but with everyone in my life.

Experience, Not Opinion

Another valuable lesson I’ve learned in sobriety is the importance of experience over opinion. In my early days, I was quick to offer my opinions on everything—whether or not I had any real experience with the matter. But over time, I’ve come to realize that opinions don’t carry much weight, especially when they aren’t grounded in personal experience.

Now, instead of offering opinions, I share my experience, strength, and hope with others. I find that this approach is much more helpful, as it allows me to connect with others on a deeper level. Rather than telling someone what I think they should do, I share what has worked for me in similar situations. This has become a cornerstone of how I interact with others, both inside and outside of A.A.

Living in the Present: One Day at a Time

Managing sobriety has become much easier with time, but I never take it for granted. I’ve learned that the key to long-term sobriety is to live in the present and take life one day at a time. I don’t worry about what tomorrow will bring or how I’ll handle future challenges. Instead, I focus on staying sober today and taking the steps I need to maintain my physical, emotional, and spiritual health.

Some of the steps I take to maintain my sobriety include attending regular A.A. meetings, staying connected with my sponsor, and engaging in daily spiritual practices like meditation and prayer. I also make time for self-care, whether it’s going to the gym, spending quality time with my family, or going on holidays to recharge.

A Life Larger Than Imagined

When I first came to A.A., my goal was simple: to stop drinking and regain control of my life. But what I received was so much more than just sobriety. Over the past twelve years, I’ve built a life that is larger and more fulfilling than I ever could have imagined. I’ve deepened my relationships with my family, expanded my spiritual practice, explored the world, and learned to live with integrity, humility, and self-sufficiency.

Sobriety has given me the freedom to live fully, and I hope that by sharing my experience, I can inspire others to see the possibilities that await them. Whether you’re just starting your journey or have been in recovery for years, know that there is always room for growth, discovery, and joy.

Conclusion: Building a Life Worth Living

Sobriety is not just about staying away from alcohol—it’s about building a life worth living. It’s about creating meaningful relationships, taking care of yourself, and embracing the opportunities that come your way. For me, the past twelve years have been a testament to the power of the A.A. program and the strength that comes from living one day at a time.

If you’re reading this and you’re struggling with alcohol or trying to find your way in recovery, I want to offer you hope. You too can build a life that is rich, fulfilling, and full of possibilities. All it takes is a willingness to stay sober, one day at a time, and a commitment to taking the necessary steps. I wish you all the best in your journey, and I hope that you can create something beautiful out of your life, just as I have.
