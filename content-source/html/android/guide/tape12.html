Step 11: Sought through prayer and meditation to improve our conscious contact with God as we understood Him, praying only for knowledge of His will for us and the power to carry that out.

Step 11 is where the spiritual aspect of the Twelve Step program really deepens. It invites us to improve our conscious contact with God, or a Higher Power, through prayer and meditation. While the earlier steps help us clear away the wreckage of the past and work on our character defects, Step 11 focuses on building an ongoing relationship with our Higher Power. It’s about staying connected, seeking guidance, and learning to trust in something greater than ourselves. For me, Step 11 is not just something I do once a day—it’s something I practice throughout the day, every day, as a way to keep myself grounded, centered, and spiritually fit.

Prayer and Meditation: A Two-Way Conversation
My understanding of Step 11 is that it involves two essential practices: prayer and meditation. Prayer is how I talk to my Higher Power, and meditation is how I listen. In the early days of my recovery, I struggled with both. I wasn’t sure how to pray, and I found it difficult to sit quietly and meditate. But over time, I’ve come to realize that prayer doesn’t have to be formal or elaborate. It’s simply talking to my Higher Power in an honest, open way. Sometimes my prayers are as simple as asking for guidance or expressing gratitude. Other times, they’re more reflective, where I ask for the strength to do what’s right or the wisdom to see a situation clearly.

Meditation, on the other hand, is about listening—about quieting my mind and being open to whatever my Higher Power has to say. In the beginning, meditation was challenging because my mind was constantly racing. I couldn’t sit still, and my thoughts would wander. But as I continued to practice, I found that meditation became easier. It didn’t have to be perfect. Some days, just sitting quietly for a few minutes and trying to focus on my breath was enough. Over time, I began to feel a sense of peace during these moments of stillness. And eventually, I started to hear that quiet, inner voice of my Higher Power guiding me.

Using Step 11 Throughout the Day
One of the most valuable lessons I’ve learned about Step 11 is that it can be practiced at any time of the day. In the morning, I start my day with prayer, asking for guidance and direction. I ask my Higher Power to help me align my will with theirs, and to guide me in doing the next right thing. Throughout the day, I try to maintain that conscious contact by staying aware of my thoughts and actions. When I feel myself slipping into selfishness, fear, or resentment, I stop and pray for help. Sometimes, I’ll take a few minutes to meditate, even if it’s just sitting quietly in my car or taking a walk.

At night, before bed, I use Step 11 to reflect on my day. I take a personal inventory, looking at where I’ve fallen short and where I’ve done well. I ask myself whether I’ve been selfish, dishonest, resentful, or afraid. If I’ve hurt anyone, I make a mental note to address it the next day. But I don’t allow myself to get bogged down in guilt or self-pity. If there’s something that’s bothering me, I pray for guidance, and if it can wait, I plan to talk to my sponsor the next day. This nightly reflection helps me stay accountable, but it also gives me the chance to let go of any worries or regrets before I go to sleep.

Meditation as Listening: A Gradual Process
In the beginning, I wasn’t sure what meditation really meant. But I was told that just sitting in a meeting, listening to other people share their stories, was a form of meditation. The word "meditate" means to focus, and in those early days, focusing on the words of others was the best form of meditation I could manage. Over time, as I became more comfortable with the idea of a Higher Power and developed a stronger connection, I found that I could sit quietly and listen for that guidance. It wasn’t always a voice or a clear message—sometimes it was just a feeling, a sense that I was on the right path, or that something needed to change.

I came to believe that this power had been guiding me long before I ever picked up my first drink, but I had been too consumed with self-will to notice. It wasn’t until I worked through the steps and began surrendering my will to a Higher Power that I became consciously aware of this guidance. It’s not always obvious or immediate, but the more I practice Step 11, the more I trust that my Higher Power is always there, helping me make better decisions and live a more peaceful life.

Different Paths to Spirituality
One of the things I love about Step 11 is that it’s deeply personal and adaptable. Everyone in the fellowship has their own individual journey with this step. Some people find spiritual connection through religious institutions, while others explore different spiritual practices. There’s no one "right" way to practice Step 11—it’s about finding what works for you. My sponsor always reminded me that as long as I kept Alcoholics Anonymous above all, I wouldn’t pick up a drink, and my life would continue to get better. That advice has stayed with me. I don’t worry about having the perfect spiritual practice. I focus on staying connected to my Higher Power in whatever way works for me.

Praying for Knowledge of God’s Will
The second part of Step 11 is about seeking knowledge of God’s will and the power to carry it out. For a long time, I didn’t fully understand what this meant. I used to pray for things I wanted—a better job, more money, a bigger house. But over time, I realized that those prayers weren’t in line with the spiritual growth I was seeking. Now, I pray for direction, for guidance in doing the next right thing. I don’t ask for specific outcomes; I ask for the wisdom to know what’s right and the strength to do it.

Sometimes, doing God’s will means focusing on the simple, everyday tasks—doing the dishes, taking a walk, going to a meeting. When I’m confused or overwhelmed, I remind myself to just do the next right thing, no matter how small. There’s always something I can do that moves me closer to peace, whether it’s a small act of kindness or just taking care of myself. By focusing on doing the next right thing, I’ve found that I no longer need to control every aspect of my life. I trust that my Higher Power is guiding me, and that as long as I’m doing my part, everything will work out as it’s meant to.

Spiritual Growth Through Prayer and Meditation
Over time, I’ve noticed that the practice of Step 11 has led to significant changes in my life. For example, I used to pray for specific things like quitting smoking or losing weight. But as I practiced Step 11, I found that those changes started happening naturally. One day, during meditation, the thought came to me that I could live a healthier life. I started going to the gym and running, and I managed to shed a lot of weight. I feel better physically, mentally, and spiritually. I’ve also found that by praying for guidance and staying connected to my Higher Power, I’ve become more open to positive changes in all areas of my life.

The Importance of Nightly Inventory
Before I go to bed, I always take time to reflect on my day. This nightly inventory helps me stay accountable and keeps me grounded in my spiritual practice. I ask myself where I’ve been selfish, dishonest, or fearful. I think about whether I’ve hurt anyone or acted out of resentment. But I don’t dwell on these things. If I’ve made mistakes, I acknowledge them, pray for guidance, and plan to address them the next day. I don’t let guilt or self-pity take over. This nightly practice helps me end each day with a sense of peace and prepares me to start fresh in the morning.

Staying Connected to a Higher Power
Throughout the day, I remind myself that I’m not running my life anymore—my Higher Power is. This shift in perspective has made a huge difference in my ability to stay calm and focused, even in challenging situations. As long as I stay connected to my Higher Power, I know that I can handle whatever comes my way. It’s not always easy, but by practicing Step 11 daily, I’ve found a sense of serenity that I never thought possible.

As the saying goes, “Faith without works is dead.” Step 11 is about putting faith into action, not just once a day, but throughout the day. By seeking conscious contact with our Higher Power and staying open to guidance, we can continue to grow spiritually and live a life of peace and purpose.
