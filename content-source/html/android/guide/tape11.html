Step 10: Continued to take personal inventory and when we were wrong promptly admitted it.

Step 10 marks the beginning of the "maintenance steps" in the Twelve Step program. After the deep, soul-searching work of the earlier steps and the healing power of making amends in Steps 8 and 9, Step 10 shifts our focus to ongoing personal growth. It’s about living each day in the spiritual world, where we continue to monitor our behavior and attitudes, quickly correct our mistakes, and remain open to change. The work doesn’t stop just because we’ve completed the first nine steps—if anything, Step 10 reminds us that the real work is continuous. If we’re not growing, we’re stagnating, and in recovery, stagnation can be dangerous. That’s why Step 10 is so crucial—it’s about daily maintenance, ensuring that we don’t slip back into old behaviors or attitudes that could lead us toward a relapse.

Maintaining Spiritual Progress
The purpose of Step 10 is to ensure that we remain spiritually fit. It’s easy to fall back into old habits of selfishness, dishonesty, resentment, and fear, even after we’ve made significant progress in our recovery. Step 10 suggests that we continue to take personal inventory, constantly looking for character defects and addressing them as soon as they arise. This step is not just about recognizing our defects but about taking immediate action to correct them. It’s about practicing vigilance and humility in our daily lives.

For me, Step 10 has become a way of life. When I first started working this step, I had to be very intentional about it. I would set aside time each day to reflect on my behavior and identify any wrongs I had committed. But as I continued to practice this step, it became more natural. Now, I’m much more attuned to my thoughts and actions throughout the day, and I can often catch myself in the moment when a defect of character begins to surface.

Monitoring for Selfishness, Dishonesty, Resentment, and Fear
The Big Book of Alcoholics Anonymous specifically mentions four key defects that we need to watch for: selfishness, dishonesty, resentment, and fear. These are often the root causes of our troubles, and they can easily creep back into our lives if we’re not careful. When I notice any of these defects cropping up, I try to address them immediately. I don’t let them fester or grow. Instead, I pause, reflect, and ask my Higher Power to help me remove them.

For example, when selfishness shows up, it often takes the form of wanting things to go my way or feeling entitled to certain outcomes. When I recognize this, I remind myself that I’m not the center of the universe and that life doesn’t always unfold according to my plans. When dishonesty appears—whether it’s a small lie or a more significant deception—I stop and ask myself why I’m being dishonest. What am I afraid of? What am I trying to protect? The same goes for resentment and fear. By monitoring these defects, I can catch them before they cause more harm.

Using Prayer and Fellowship for Support
One of the most effective tools I’ve found for working Step 10 is prayer. As soon as I notice a character defect resurfacing, I stop and pray for guidance. I ask my Higher Power to help me deal with the defect and to guide me toward better actions. This practice has helped me become more grounded and less reactive. Sometimes, when I’m particularly struggling with a defect, I’ll also call my sponsor and share what’s going on. Just talking about it with someone else can help me gain clarity and perspective. My sponsor often shares his own experiences with me, and together, we can work through the issue.

This sharing is a crucial part of Step 10 for me. It’s not about telling someone what happened—it’s about sharing openly and honestly, without shame or fear of judgment. And when I share with my sponsor, he’s able to offer me valuable feedback without criticizing me. Sometimes, he can see things that I’m too close to recognize. Other times, just the act of sharing helps me process the situation and see things more clearly.

Turning Bad Days into Bad Hours
One of the most profound gifts of working Step 10 is that it has drastically improved my ability to handle everyday challenges. In the past, a bad situation would ruin my entire day. I’d dwell on it, let it fester, and allow it to affect my mood, behavior, and interactions with others. But through practicing Step 10, I’ve learned to deal with these situations much more effectively and quickly. Now, I don’t have bad days—I have bad hours. If something goes wrong, I can pause, reflect, and take action to turn things around. I no longer let small setbacks derail my entire day. Instead, I use Step 10 to course-correct and get back on track.

This shift in perspective has been life-changing. It allows me to maintain a sense of peace and serenity, even when things don’t go my way. No matter what happens, I know that I have the tools to deal with it and move forward. As the saying goes, "I can start my 24 hours at any time of the day." If I find myself struggling, I don’t have to wait until the next morning to reset. I can start over in that moment, taking stock of where I am and making the necessary adjustments.

Prompt Action and Admitting When We’re Wrong
A key aspect of Step 10 is taking prompt action when we’re wrong. This means admitting our mistakes as soon as we recognize them, rather than letting them linger. Sometimes, I don’t immediately see when I’m wrong, but I can tell that something is off. When I’m disturbed or agitated, I take that as a sign that something isn’t right, even if I can’t pinpoint the exact issue. In these moments, I reach out to my sponsor or a close friend in the fellowship. By sharing what’s going on, I can often gain clarity and recognize where I’ve gone wrong.

I’ve also learned the importance of sharing, not telling. When I share my experience with others, I’m more likely to get my message across in a constructive way. But when I try to tell people what to do, I often come across as controlling or judgmental. Step 10 has taught me to approach communication with humility and openness, which has improved my relationships and interactions with others.

Practicing Honesty and Integrity in Daily Life
Step 10 has also helped me practice honesty and integrity in my day-to-day life. For example, there have been several times when I’ve noticed that a cashier at the supermarket forgot to ring up an item. In the past, I might have shrugged it off and seen it as a small victory. But now, I can’t live with that kind of dishonesty. I always go back and pay for the item, even if it’s inconvenient. These small actions help me stay aligned with my values and prevent small defects from growing into bigger problems. I like to "nip stuff in the bud," as I’ve learned that letting small defects slide can lead to bigger issues down the road.

The Power of Service
One of the most important lessons I’ve learned from Step 10 is that when all else fails, working with another alcoholic never fails. When I’m having a tough day and struggling with my defects, reaching out to someone else in the fellowship can shift my perspective. Sometimes, just listening to someone else’s struggles or sharing my own experience can turn my thinking around. It reminds me that I’m not alone and that I have a community of support. And often, the act of helping someone else is enough to pull me out of my own head and back into the present moment.

Serenity Through Acceptance
Ultimately, Step 10 is about maintaining the serenity that comes from living in acceptance. When we trade expectations for acceptance, we find peace. Step 10 helps me live in that space of acceptance, knowing that I will make mistakes but that I also have the tools to correct them. It’s a lifelong process, but by practicing Step 10, I’ve found that I can navigate life’s challenges with more grace and resilience.
